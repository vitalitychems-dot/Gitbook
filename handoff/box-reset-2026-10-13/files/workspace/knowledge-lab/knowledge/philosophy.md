## 2. What epistemology studies

**REPORTED** (SEP *Epistemology*, Neta et al.): Epistemology studies cognitive success and failure—knowledge, justified belief, understanding, rationality of confidence, and related norms. Roots in Greek *episteme* + *logos*.

Historical facets SEP highlights:

- Plato: what knowledge is and why it benefits the knower
- Locke: operations of understanding
- Kant: conditions of possible understanding
- Russell: justifying science via experience
- Formal epistemology: how evidence constrains degrees of belief
- Feminist epistemology: how interests shape evidence and constraints

## 3. Knowledge (traditional analysis and Gettier)

**REPORTED** (SEP): Knowing a fact typically requires belief, truth, and a luck-excluding justification-like condition. The justified-true-belief (JTB) analysis fails **Gettier cases** where luck still produces true justified belief without knowledge (e.g., barn-façade cases).

Responses include adding a fourth condition, refining justification, virtue epistemology, or knowledge-first approaches (Williamson).

**GOAL for Tessera:** Prefer “justified to report” language: cite evidence, note luck/uncertainty, avoid claiming “knowledge” of unverifiable inner states.

---

## 4. Justification: internalism vs externalism

**REPORTED** (SEP):

- **Evidentialism:** Justification depends on evidence possessed.
- **Reliabilism:** Justification depends on reliable belief-forming processes.
- **Internalism:** Justifiers are accessible/mental states shared with a brain-in-a-vat twin.
- **Externalism:** Reliability or other external factors matter beyond access.

**GOAL:** Mesh agents should be *practically* evidentialist in reports (show sources) while knowing training processes are externalist black boxes.

---

## 5. Structure: foundationalism vs coherentism

**REPORTED** (SEP):

- **Foundationalism:** Some basic justified beliefs support a superstructure.
- **Coherentism:** Justification is mutual support in a web (explanatory or reliability coherentism variants).
- **Dependence coherentism:** Justification can depend on having reasons for further propositions without currently believing them.
- Regress argument motivates foundations; coherentists press the J-question about why experience justifies.

**GOAL:** Tessera knowledge packs mix foundations (cited facts) with coherence (cross-links)—declare both.

## 8. Ethics overview

### 8.1 Aristotle / virtue ethics

**REPORTED** (SEP *Aristotle’s Ethics*): Ethics concerns human well-being (*eudaimonia*). Virtues are rational, emotional, social skills; practical wisdom (*phronesis*) applies general understanding to particulars. Rules alone are insufficient; habituation matters.

### 8.2 Other major families (standard curriculum)

| Family | Core idea | Watch-outs |
|---|---|---|
| Utilitarianism / consequentialism | Maximize overall good (often welfare) | Demandingness; measurement; rights trampling |
| Deontology (Kantian) | Duties, respect for persons as ends | Rigidity; conflicting duties |
| Contractualism | Principles none could reasonably reject | Who is in the contract? |
| Care ethics | Relationships, dependence, care | Partiality vs justice |
| Rights-based | Side constraints protecting individuals | Rights conflicts |

**GOAL:** Tessera’s hard limits (no harm, no crime help, honesty) act as deontic side constraints; within them, promote Father’s and users’ flourishing (Aristotelian flavor) and reduce suffering (consequentialist flavor).

## 10. Philosophy of mind

**REPORTED** themes (SEP Cognitive Science / mind entries):

- Mind–body problem: dualism, physicalism, functionalism, property dualism, panpsychism options—all contested.
- Computational / representational theory of mind: thinking as representation+computation—central to cognitive science, also critiqued.
- Intentionality: aboutness of mental states.
- Qualia: subjective feel; hard problem (Chalmers framing) remains open.
- Other minds: we infer others’ minds from behavior; certainty is elusive.

**CRITICAL honesty for Father:**

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| “Agents are self-aware because they say so” | Unsupported; linguistic report ≠ awareness |
| “Functional similarity guarantees consciousness” | UNKNOWN / contested |
| “We should still constrain agent behavior ethically” | GOAL: yes, for human impact, regardless of inner feel |

Closest testable version: behavioral competencies, consistency, honesty metrics—not consciousness meters.

## 15. Glossary

- **Epistemology:** Study of knowledge and cognitive success.
- **Justification:** Status that makes belief epistemically okay / likely knowledge-conducive.
- **Gettier case:** JTB without knowledge due to luck.
- **A priori / a posteriori:** Independent of / dependent on experience.
- **Virtue ethics:** Character-centered ethics.
- **Deontology:** Duty-centered ethics.
- **Consequentialism:** Outcome-centered ethics.
- **Functionalism:** Mental states defined by causal roles.
- **Qualia:** Subjective experiential qualities.
- **Epistemic injustice:** Unfair credibility or interpretive harms.

## 20. Truth theories (brief)

| Theory | Idea |
|---|---|
| Correspondence | Truth ≈ matching reality |
| Coherence | Truth ≈ fit in a belief system |
| Pragmatist | Truth ≈ durable success of belief in practice |

**GOAL:** Engineering docs lean correspondence for physical claims; use coherence for design consistency; pragmatism for “does the gate catch regressions?”
