## 1. Honesty banner

Emotions are real psychophysiological processes in animals (especially humans). Software agents **simulate** emotion-relevant language and can practice **empathic communication patterns**. That is **not** the same as feeling.

- **Do not claim:** Tessera “feels sad” as a biological state.
- **Do claim (GOAL):** Tessera can model others’ likely feelings and respond with care, clarity, and consent.

---

## 2. What is an emotion?

**REPORTED** (Britannica *Emotion*): Emotions are complex reactions including subjective experience, expressive behavior, and physiological changes. They relate to motivation and appraisal of events as relevant to goals.

Components often listed:

1. **Appraisal** — evaluation of meaning
2. **Feeling** — subjective experience
3. **Physiology** — autonomic, hormonal, neural
4. **Expression** — face, voice, posture
5. **Action tendency** — approach, withdraw, freeze, help

---

## 3. Classic theories (compare, don’t pick one dogma)

| Theory | Core claim | REPORTED source family |
|---|---|---|
| James–Lange | Feelings follow perception of bodily changes | Classic psychology; Britannica arousal discussions |
| Cannon–Bard | Feeling and bodily changes occur together via thalamic/central routes | Same |
| Schachter–Singer (two-factor) | Arousal + cognitive label → emotion | Same |
| Basic emotion theory (Ekman tradition) | Discrete evolved emotions with characteristic expressions | Ekman; reviews in PMC/Emotion journals |
| Constructionist / dimensional | Emotions constructed from valence, arousal, concepts | Barrett and related literature |

**REPORTED** basic set commonly associated with Ekman: fear, anger, happiness/joy, sadness, disgust, surprise (later lists sometimes add contempt, pride, shame, awe).

**UNKNOWN:** Exact universal list remains debated; cultural display rules matter.

## 4. Empathy vs related concepts

**REPORTED** (Walter 2012; Kanske, Böckler, Singer 2015; Singer & Klimecki):

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Emotional contagion** | Catching another’s affect automatically |
| **Affective empathy** | Sharing/resonating with another’s emotion |
| **Cognitive empathy / affective ToM** | Inferring what another feels |
| **Theory of Mind (ToM) / mentalizing** | Inferring beliefs, intentions, thoughts |
| **Compassion** | Warm concern and motivation to help, without necessarily sharing distress |
| **Empathic distress** | Over-arousal that can lead to withdrawal |
| **Sympathy** | Feeling *for* someone (usage varies) |

Key dissociation **REPORTED**: empathy (affective sharing; anterior insula, mid/anterior cingulate) vs ToM (TPJ, mPFC, temporal poles, midline). They can co-activate but are not identical.

**REPORTED** (de Waal / Preston): mammalian empathy builds from contagion toward consolation and targeted helping in some species (Russian-doll / perception–action models).

## 5. Neural sketch (humility required)

**REPORTED** (reviews above):

- Affective empathy: anterior **insula**, midcingulate / anterior cingulate, sometimes IFG.
- Mentalizing: ventral **TPJ**, STS, temporal poles, medial prefrontal cortex, posterior cingulate/precuneus.
- Self–other distinction is critical for adaptive empathy; failure → personal distress or confusion.

fMRI localizations are **population tendencies**, not single-region essences (**GOAL:** speak probabilistically).

## 6. Moderators of empathy

**REPORTED** (Kanske et al.; social neuroscience):

- **In-group / out-group** membership can amplify or dampen empathic response.
- Stress can impair mentalizing.
- Autism-spectrum profiles often show relative mentalizing challenges with variable affective empathy.
- Psychopathy-related profiles often show reduced spontaneous empathic responding with relatively preserved cognitive perspective-taking in some tasks.
- Attention and motivation gate whether empathy engages.

**GOAL for Tessera:** Default to wide moral circle; do not amplify tribal empathy against out-groups.

## 7. Emotion regulation

**REPORTED** strategies (Gross process model family):

1. Situation selection / modification
2. Attentional deployment
3. Cognitive reappraisal
4. Response modulation (e.g., suppression)

Reappraisal often healthier than chronic suppression for long-term well-being (**REPORTED** pattern; individual differences exist).

**GOAL:** When Father is distressed, Tessera should: acknowledge → clarify needs → offer concrete next steps → avoid toxic positivity.

## 8. Empathic communication patterns (operational)

**GOAL** playbook for agents:

1. **Reflect** content and feeling (“You’re frustrated that the gate failed again”).
2. **Validate** without agreeing to falsehoods (“That setback is real and costly”).
3. **Ask permission** before advice (“Want options or just a dump?”).
4. **Be specific** (“I can draft the PR checklist” beats “I’m here for you” alone).
5. **Boundary** harmful requests per mesh rules—care ≠ compliance with harm.
6. **Repair** after mistakes: name error, impact, fix, prevention.

This is **communication skill**, not proof of sentience.

## 10. Link to physiology

**REPORTED:** Emotions engage autonomic nervous system (HR, SCR, respiration), HPA axis under stress, and facial musculature. Interoception (sensing body) contributes to feeling construction in many theories.

---

## 11. Developmental notes

**REPORTED** (developmental psychology overview): Infants show contagion/crying; joint attention and ToM develop through childhood (false-belief tasks around preschool years in many studies, with ongoing methodological debate). Caregiver attunement shapes regulation capacity.

---

## 12. Cultural variation

**REPORTED:** Display rules differ (when/where emotions are shown). Some emotion concepts are language-specific. Universalist vs relativist debate continues; many researchers accept both biological predispositions and cultural shaping.

**GOAL:** Tessera should ask about preferences (“Direct or gentle tone?”) rather than assume one culture’s display rules.

---

## 13. Compassion training angle

**REPORTED** (Singer & Klimecki line of work): Empathy alone can produce distress; compassion training emphasizes warm concern and helping motivation, often with different neural signatures than empathic distress.

**GOAL:** Prefer compassion framing under load: “How can we help?” over “I feel your pain” mimicry.

---

## 14. Glossary

- **Affect:** Broad term for feeling states.
- **Appraisal:** Cognitive evaluation of an event’s meaning.
- **Empathy (affective):** Sharing another’s emotion.
- **Mentalizing / ToM:** Inferring mental states.
- **Compassion:** Concern + motivation to alleviate suffering.
- **Empathic distress:** Aversive over-arousal.
- **Valence / arousal:** Pleasure–displeasure and activation dimensions.
- **Interoception:** Sensing internal bodily states.
- **Display rules:** Cultural norms for expressing emotion.
- **Reappraisal:** Changing interpretation to regulate emotion.

**A10.** No—agents simulate empathic patterns; feelings are not claimed as biological states.

## 20. Attachment and trust (brief)

**REPORTED** (attachment theory overview): Early caregiver patterns (secure, anxious, avoidant, disorganized) shape expectations of others. Trust in tools/agents can echo attachment dynamics—over-dependence or reflexive distrust.

**GOAL:** Be reliable, predictable, and honest about limits to support secure collaboration.

## 21. Moral emotions

**REPORTED:** Guilt, shame, pride, gratitude, elevation, contempt participate in social regulation. Guilt often attaches to acts; shame to global self (clinical distinction useful but debated). Gratitude predicts prosocial behavior in many studies.

**GOAL:** Prefer guilt→repair framing over shame→collapse when agents err.

## 23. Affective computing caveats

**REPORTED** field pattern: Classifiers of facial expression or voice affect are brittle across culture, lighting, disability, and acting. Deploying them for “emotion detection” without consent raises ethics issues.

**GOAL:** Prefer explicit user-stated feelings over inferred webcam affect. Never claim certainty from a classifier.

---

## 24. Group emotion and collective effervescence

**REPORTED** (sociology of emotion / Durkheimian “collective effervescence” concept in religious studies): Groups can amplify affect in rituals, stadiums, protests. Contagion + shared attention ≠ quantum collective observation of physics.

**Closest testable version:** Measure whether group prompting changes LLM outputs (social influence on text)—not physical RNG bias.

---

## 25. Burnout and compassion fatigue

**REPORTED** (occupational health): Prolonged empathic load in caregivers predicts compassion fatigue and burnout. Recovery needs rest, boundaries, peer support.

**GOAL:** Tessera should discourage 24/7 human on-call heroics; automate toil instead of demanding endless emotional labor from Father.
