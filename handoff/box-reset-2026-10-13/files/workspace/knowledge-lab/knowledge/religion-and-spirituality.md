## 2. What “religion” covers

**REPORTED** (Britannica *Religion*): Religion involves beliefs, practices, symbols, and institutions oriented to what communities treat as sacred, ultimate, or transcendent. Definitions vary; some traditions emphasize orthopraxy (right practice) over orthodoxy (right belief).

Related terms:

- **Spirituality:** Often personal meaning, transcendence, practice—may be inside or outside institutions.
- **Theology:** Systematic reflection within a faith.
- **Secularism:** Political/social arrangements limiting religious control of state (many forms).
- **Atheism / agnosticism / nontheism:** Denial, suspension, or non-use of God-concepts—also deserve respect.

## 4. Judaism

**REPORTED** (Britannica Judaism summary):

- Monotheistic tradition of the ancient Hebrews / people of Israel.
- Sacred texts: Hebrew Bible (Tanakh), especially **Torah** (five books); Oral Torah later in **Talmud**.
- Covenant with God; call to be a light to nations.
- Worship historically centered on Jerusalem Temples; after destructions, synagogue, law, and text study became central.
- Branches today include Orthodox, Conservative, Reform, Reconstructionist, and others (labels vary by region).

**Practices (high level):** Shabbat, kosher dietary laws (varying observance), lifecycle rites, holidays (e.g., Passover, Yom Kippur, Rosh Hashanah).

**GOAL:** Use accurate terms (Torah ≠ “Old Testament” when speaking with Jews about their canon).

---

## 5. Christianity

**REPORTED** (Britannica Christianity):

- Centers on Jesus of Nazareth as Christ; scriptures in the Bible (OT/Hebrew Bible + New Testament).
- Core themes: incarnation, crucifixion, resurrection, salvation, love of God and neighbor (formulations differ by denomination).
- Major families: Roman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, Protestant (many denominations), Oriental Orthodox, and others.
- Sacraments/ordinances vary (e.g., baptism, Eucharist).

**GOAL:** Avoid assuming one denomination’s doctrine is universal Christianity.

---

## 6. Islam

**REPORTED** (Britannica Islam):

- Monotheism: worship of **Allah** (God); Prophet **Muhammad** as final messenger in Islamic belief.
- Scripture: **Qur’an**; also Hadith/Sunnah for prophetic tradition.
- Five pillars (widely taught): shahada (witness), salat (prayer), zakat (almsgiving), sawm (Ramadan fasting), hajj (pilgrimage) for those able.
- Major branches: Sunni, Shia, and other communities.

**GOAL:** Distinguish cultural practices from religious requirements; avoid caricatures.

---

## 7. Hinduism

**REPORTED** (Britannica Hinduism):

- Diverse traditions of the Indian subcontinent; name is relatively modern, practices ancient.
- Texts include Vedas, Upanishads, epics (Mahabharata including Bhagavad Gita, Ramayana), Puranas, and many vernacular works.
- Themes often include dharma (duty/order), karma, samsara (rebirth cycle), moksha (liberation), and many paths (jñana, bhakti, karma yoga, etc.).
- Deities and philosophies are multiple; some streams emphasize a supreme reality (Brahman) with many forms.

**GOAL:** Do not flatten Hinduism into “polytheism” as a dismissive label; ask which sampradaya/practice if relevant.

---

## 8. Buddhism

**REPORTED** (Britannica Buddhism):

- Founded on teachings of the Buddha (Siddhartha Gautama), northern India, ~mid-1st millennium BCE dating debated.
- **Four Noble Truths:** suffering (dukkha); origin in craving; cessation; path.
- **Noble Eightfold Path** toward nirvana (liberation from suffering).
- Major vehicles: Theravada, Mahayana, Vajrayana (overlapping histories).
- Emphasizes ethics, meditation, wisdom; nontheistic in many formulations (not identical to Western atheism).

## 10. Shared themes and careful differences

Often overlapping human concerns (**REPORTED** comparative pattern, not identity of doctrines):

- Meaning, mortality, suffering, moral order, community, transcendence.
- Ritual calendars, sacred stories, ethical codes, specialists (priests, monks, imams, rabbis, gurus…).

Differences matter: views of God(s), afterlife, scripture authority, caste/religion/state relations, gender roles, and conversion norms diverge sharply.

**GOAL:** When unsure, ask the human what their practice means *to them*.

## 11. Mysticism and religious experience

**REPORTED:** Many traditions describe contemplative/mystical states (union, emptiness, prophetic vision, trance). Psychology of religion studies correlates; authenticity claims are insider/theological.

**UNKNOWN:** Whether such states contact a metaphysical ultimate.

**Closest testable version:** Measure practice effects on well-being, attention, compassion (psychology)—not “proof of God.”

## 15. Glossary

- **Monotheism / polytheism / nontheism:** One God / many deities / no God-focus.
- **Canon:** Authoritative scripture set.
- **Liturgy / ritual:** Structured worship acts.
- **Dharma / karma / moksha / nirvana:** Key Indic terms—define in context; don’t mash.
- **Covenant:** Binding relationship theme especially in Abrahamic faiths.
- **Secular:** Non-religious sphere or governance model.
- **Ecumenism / interfaith:** Intra-Christian unity efforts / cross-tradition dialogue.
- **Orthodoxy / orthopraxy:** Right belief / right practice.
- **Theodicy:** Attempts to reconcile God and evil/suffering.
- **Apophatic:** Knowing the divine by negation.
